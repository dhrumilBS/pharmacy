# Brand name options: pharmacy management software

Prepared 13 Oct 2026. This is a working shortlist for the product owner. Nothing here is cleared for use yet. Domain and conflict checks are a first pass and are **indicative only**. Verify them with a registrar and a trademark professional before you adopt a name.

The product is browser-based software for Indian medical stores (primary), pharmacy chains and hospital pharmacies. It covers FEFO billing, expiry and stock control, distributor bill import, GST reports, Tally export, multi-store and IPD/TPA. The buyer is a sceptical owner-pharmacist, often switching from old desktop software. They want a name that sounds dependable and simple, not clever. The current owner company name and anything that sounds like it are excluded.

## Selection criteria

1. **2–3 syllables.** Easy to say over the phone and to spell from hearing, in English and Hindi/Gujarati/Marathi at least.
2. **No awkward meaning** in Hindi, Gujarati, Marathi, Bengali, Tamil or Telugu (obvious checks only, listed per name below).
3. **Signals the counter.** Medicine, accuracy, records, expiry/stock control or the capsule-check idea, without being generic ("Pharma Soft", "Medi Bill").
4. **Pairs with the logo** (a capsule-check tick on a blue→teal tile, or capsule-plus) and works as "<Name> Pharmacy" / "<Name> for hospitals".
5. **Domain room.** .com or .in plausibly available.
6. **No visible conflict** with an Indian pharmacy/health software, an e-pharmacy or a large pharma brand.
7. **Calm tone.** No hype words, no compliance guarantees implied by the name.

## Method (what was actually checked)

- About 90 names were brainstormed: coined blends, real words, compounds, Hindi/Sanskrit-rooted and Latin-rooted. About 75 went through DNS checks and roughly 25 through web conflict searches.
- **Domains:** `nslookup <name>.com` and `<name>.in` against 8.8.8.8. *NX* = NXDOMAIN, so the name is possibly unregistered. *Resolves* = registered (could be in use, parked or for sale). A few were also checked with `curl -sI`. **Indicative only. Verify with a registrar.** A domain can be registered without DNS, so NX does not prove it is available.
- **Conflicts:** web searches for each shortlisted name (exact name, "+ pharmacy", "+ app"). No result means nothing showed up in a general search. It does **not** mean the name is trademark-clear.
- **Trademark:** the IP India public search needs a captcha and could not be run. See next steps.

## Top 3 recommendations

### 1. Kapstik
- **Why:** it turns the planned logo into the name, so you never have to explain the mark. It is coined, so it has the best chance of a clean trademark in Class 9/42. It carries no regional language bias, which matters when selling into Gujarat, Maharashtra and the South at once. Two syllables, easy on a phone call.
- **Risk:** spelling. People may hear "Capstick" or "Capstik", so buy the obvious misspellings (capstik, kapstick). It does not say "pharmacy" on its own, so always use it with the descriptor ("Kapstik Pharmacy Software").
- **Sample tagline:** *Bill faster. Waste less.* / *Every strip, checked.*

### 2. MedLekha
- **Why:** *lekha/lekka* (accounts, records) is understood across Hindi, Marathi, Gujarati, Bengali, Telugu and Kannada. It fits the calm, records-focused tone the site already uses for H1 registers, GST and stock ledgers, and helps without promising compliance. Both domains looked unregistered.
- **Risk:** half-Hindi blends can look informal to hospital IT buyers. Lekha is a common given name. Check that it reads fine in Tamil, where "lekha" is a name, not a word for accounts.
- **Sample tagline:** *Your counter, in order.* / *Stock, bills and registers in one place.*

### 3. DawaSahi
- **Why:** it speaks the owner's language. "Right medicine" is exactly what the counter worries about (wrong batch, wrong rate, expired strip), and *sahi* = "signature" in Marathi/Gujarati adds a "signed off" sense. The tick in the logo reads as *sahi*.
- **Risk:** it has a Hindi-belt flavour, and in South India "dawa" is understood but not native. Spelling varies (dawa / dava / dawai), so register the variants. "Sahi" can sound like a guarantee of correctness, so keep the copy calm ("helps you pick the right batch"). The crowded "Dawa-" space in the Gulf and Africa (Dawaai, DawaDose, DawaPay) matters only if you expand abroad.
- **Sample tagline:** *Right batch. Right bill.*

**If none of the three suit, the runner-up is KapSahi** (combines the logo link of #1 with the meaning of #3, and both domains looked unregistered).

## Next steps

1. **Registrar check (this week):** check the top 3 and KapSahi on a registrar (GoDaddy / Namecheap / BigRock / .in registry). Look at .com, .in and .co.in, plus common misspellings (e.g. capstik, kapstick; davasahi, dawasahee). Buy defensively once a name is chosen.
2. **Trademark search (before any public use):** commission a professional search on IP India (and WIPO Global Brand Database) in:
   - **Class 9** (downloadable/recorded software)
   - **Class 42** (SaaS, software as a service)
   - **Class 35** (retail/business management, billing and accounting services)
   - **Class 44** (only if the brand will cover pharmacy/medical services)
   Ask for phonetic matches too (e.g. Kapstik vs Capstick/Kapstone, Tikmed vs TikoMed/TEKMED).
3. **Social handles:** check Instagram, YouTube, X, LinkedIn page, Facebook page and a WhatsApp Business display name. Also check the Play Store/App Store for any app of the same name.
4. **Quick owner survey (5–10 pharmacists, different states):** read each name aloud over the phone. Ask them to spell it, say what it suggests, and pick a favourite. Include at least one Gujarati, one Marathi and one Tamil/Telugu-speaking owner to catch meaning or pronunciation problems.
5. **Logo test:** mock the capsule-check tile with each of the top 3 wordmarks at favicon size (16px), on an invoice header and on a WhatsApp profile picture.
6. **After sign-off:** the site copy and footer (`index.html`) still need the chosen name added. Product claims stay marked `data-confirm` until the owner confirms them.

## US + India shortlist (follow-up, 13 Oct 2026)

**New requirement:** the name must work in both the **US and India**. It should be easy to say and spell in both, use English roots rather than Hindi-only words, and carry no awkward meaning in either country.

- **Domains:** about 90 candidates checked with `nslookup`. Most short English names (Batchly, Shelfwise, Pharmly, Rxly, Dosely, Expiro…) are taken on .com.
- **Conflicts:** web searches for the exact names below found no current brand.
- **Indicative only:** a professional search at the **USPTO and IP India** (Classes 9 and 42, plus 35 and 44) is required before adopting any name.

| Name | Idea | US read | India read | .com / .in | Conflicts found | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **RxTik** | Rx (prescription) + tick | "R-X-tick": every pharmacist knows Rx | Rx is printed on every Indian prescription | free? / free? | None | Matches the capsule-check logo. "Rx" names are common in US pharmacy software (PioneerRx, BestRx), so it fits the category. Faint TikTok echo |
| **CapWise** | capsule + wise | Plain English, instantly readable | Plain English, readable | free? / free? | None exact (near: Campwise, KapitalWise; unrelated) | Calm and trustworthy. "Cap" also means price cap in the US, which is neutral to slightly positive |

| **Rxtiva** | Rx + "active" | "R-X-TEE-va" | Same | free? / free? | None | Very protectable coined word; carries no meaning |

### Recommendation (US + India)
1. **RxTik.** "Rx" is universal for pharmacists in both countries, the tick matches the capsule-check logo, it's short, both domains look free, and no conflicts were found. Tagline: *"Right batch. Every bill."*
2. **CapWise.** The most natural English name; it reads as calm and trustworthy to owners in both markets.
3. **Kapstik.** Keep as an India-first option. Run a quick US listening test for the ChapStick association before choosing it for both markets.
